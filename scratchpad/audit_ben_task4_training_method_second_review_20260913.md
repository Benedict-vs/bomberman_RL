# Ben task-4 training methods: independent second review (2026-09-13)

Scope: static inspection of Ben's plateau tooling, task-4 checkpoint handling and related BEN.md/CODEX.md statements. No training, game evaluations, installations, external-agent implementation reads, or agent/doc edits. This report is written incrementally; findings below concern what the implementation establishes, not a presumed cause of poor play.

## Confirmed findings

1. **The plateau label does not establish convergence or training futility.** `tools/plateau_check.py:64–84` checks only whether every recent adjacent mean-score difference is below 0.10 (default patience 2, lines 39–41). It uses no confidence interval, equivalence/futility bound, paired comparison, or fragility check. Declines satisfy the criterion; a single rebound above the threshold yields `still_improving` even if the overall trajectory falls. Illustrative arithmetic, not an executed experiment: scores 3.0→2.0→2.2 yield `still_improving`; 2.0→2.09→2.18 yield `plateau`. These labels describe a local threshold rule, not evidence that further training can or cannot help.

2. **Safety reporting is not automated safety termination.** `tools/plateau_check.py:69–84` prints a suicide-regression flag, but returns status solely from score plateau. `tools/train_ben_task4_kill_reward_plateau_pilot.sh:84–97` handles only status 0, and even that stops only with `BM_PLATEAU_STOP=1` (default 0, line 9). Status 2 errors are also not distinguished from continuation. Thus BEN.md:29's safety-termination statement and BEN.md:257's proposed stop/discard workflow are not enforced by this runner. Human intervention may implement them; its occurrence cannot be inferred from this code.

3. **“Continuation” preserves policy weights, not training state.** `agent_code/ben_task4/train.py:503–525` saves only the online-network state dictionary. Setup recreates the target as a copy of the online network, Adam, empty replay, and zeroed environment/optimization counters (`244–273`). The narrowly inspected loader confirms that training reads `LOAD_MODEL_FILE` and loads network weights (`callbacks.py:314–333`); training also reseeds Python/NumPy/Torch (`305–308`). Each new process repeats the 5,000-transition replay warm-up (`train.py:65,1095–1096`). Epsilon resets too, but **does not jump upward in this pilot**: `FINE_TUNE_CRATE_WAIT=True` (`callbacks.py:23`) makes its start and end both 0.05 (`train.py:76–85`). Saved files are valid inference checkpoints; they are insufficient to resume the original training trajectory. Possible reset-induced forgetting or lost learning opportunity remains a hypothesis, not an observed cause.

4. **The phased pilot mixes training duration, restarts and opponent changes.** The runner launches a fresh process for each 250-episode phase (`35–47`), chains the preceding final weights (`69–82`), and alternates Mixed/External opponents (`74–81`). Therefore adjacent evaluations cannot isolate a duration effect under a fixed training distribution. Both reward arms share these interventions, so this does **not** invalidate their comparison under the specified schedule. But equal episode counts do not establish equal optimizer-update counts: learning happens per collected transition after warm-up (`train.py:1087–1096,1117–1136`), and episode lengths can differ. BEN.md:29's control addresses an equal-duration restarted schedule, not necessarily equal additional updates. Neither the schedule nor a negative result tests uninterrupted continuation with preserved replay/Adam/target state.

5. **“Finished” need not mean plateau, and “no plateau” need not mean progress.** The loop always has at most four phases (`pilot.sh:72`); default observation mode ends at that budget even when the detector returns `still_improving`. With stopping enabled, the two arms can terminate at different phases, hence different cumulative durations and last training fields. A comparison of their respective endpoints would then need to identify that difference. No source-model evaluation enters `eval_files` (`10,66,71`), so the detector cannot determine whether the schedule improves on its starting incumbent. The script refuses existing training artifacts (`28–31`); it does not automatically resume an interrupted schedule. These are design limits, not evidence that an actual run stopped incorrectly.

6. **The duration check supports a local observation, not general futility.** The related `tools/evaluate_ben_task4_duration_checkpoints.sh:17–40` selects episodes 200/500/800/1000 for seed 11, uses 100 evaluation rounds at each, then applies the same detector. Unlike the phased pilot, these are checkpoints from one run; they should not be accused of intervening training resets. Nonetheless, the detector ignores episode spacing, older points outside its final window, and uncertainty. It also trusts supplied order and CSV contents without checking sample size, round pairing, greedy provenance, model identity or metadata (`plateau_check.py:17–32,44–64`). Thus BEN.md:54's recorded late score differences support “little observed late change on this validation sample”; BEN.md:56's prediction that longer training is probably useless is an extrapolation. CODEX.md:295–308 can set a resource-allocation rule, but cannot turn that rule into a convergence result. This review did not revalidate the documented measurements or historical artifacts.

## Supported conclusion and limits

Stopping a specific unsuccessful pilot or withholding promotion is compatible with a bounded experiment budget. It is different from establishing that more training cannot help. BEN.md:543–544 reports a significantly worse particular 7,000-total-episode endpoint; accepting that report does not establish that every intermediate/later checkpoint, training seed, or uninterrupted continuation would fail. Likewise, failure to demonstrate a reward advantage is not demonstration of zero effect (BEN.md:34–37 itself gives a score interval extending to +0.226).

The strongest defensible methods conclusion is: these are limited checkpoint/schedule tests with an operational stopping heuristic. They cannot explain **why** Ben fails against external agents or establish an attainable-performance ceiling. Restart effects, training-field adaptation and insufficient learning duration remain possible explanations requiring evidence; none is proven here. CODEX.md:221–222 already correctly limits `still_improving` to noisy phase changes.

For the parent write-up, retain the negative measured endpoints and budget decisions, label the detector as a heuristic, distinguish weight-initialized fine-tuning from full-state resumption, and avoid interpreting “no demonstrated gain” as “continued training disproven.” A future duration claim would need an explicit continuation protocol and uncertainty-aware, multi-training-seed evidence; this is an evidentiary requirement, not a recommendation to launch work now.
